# Stakeholders

## 1. Purpose

This document identifies the major stakeholders of the Art Marketplace Platform and describes their high-level relationship with the project.

Stakeholder identification provides a foundation for understanding user needs, responsibilities, expectations, and potential project constraints.

Detailed user roles, permissions, workflows, and requirements will be defined during the Requirements Engineering phase.

---

## 2. Stakeholder Categories

The project stakeholders are divided into the following high-level categories:

* Primary stakeholders.
* Secondary stakeholders.
* Project and technical stakeholders.
* Future stakeholders.

---

## 3. Primary Stakeholders

### 3.1 Customers

Customers are users who browse, discover, and potentially purchase artworks through the platform.

Their high-level interests include:

* Easy artwork discovery.
* Clear artwork information.
* Search and filtering.
* Secure account management.
* Simple purchasing workflows.
* Order visibility.
* Reliable platform performance.
* Protection of personal information.

Customers are one of the primary target user groups of the platform.

---

### 3.2 Artists

Artists are users who use the platform to showcase and potentially sell their artworks.

Their high-level interests include:

* Creating and managing an artist profile.
* Presenting artworks professionally.
* Managing artwork information.
* Managing artwork availability.
* Receiving and managing orders.
* Reaching potential customers.
* Protecting their content and information.

Artists are one of the core stakeholder groups because the platform's marketplace model depends on the availability and presentation of artworks.

---

### 3.3 Administrators

Administrators are responsible for managing and supervising the platform.

Their high-level interests include:

* Managing users.
* Managing artists.
* Managing artworks.
* Managing categories.
* Managing orders.
* Monitoring platform activity.
* Enforcing platform policies.
* Maintaining platform integrity and security.

The exact administrative responsibilities and permissions will be defined during the Requirements and System Design phases.

---

## 4. Project and Technical Stakeholders

### 4.1 Project Owner / Developer

The project owner and developer is responsible for:

* Defining project direction.
* Making project decisions.
* Learning and applying software engineering practices.
* Designing and implementing the system.
* Maintaining project documentation.
* Evaluating technologies.
* Testing and validating the system.
* Managing project scope.
* Deploying and maintaining the application.

Because this is an educational project, the developer also acts as the primary learner throughout the software lifecycle.

---

### 4.2 Future Development Contributors

The project may eventually involve additional developers or contributors.

Their interests may include:

* Clear documentation.
* Understandable architecture.
* Maintainable code.
* Consistent development practices.
* Reproducible development environments.
* Clear contribution guidelines.
* Reliable testing and deployment processes.

The repository should therefore be structured so that another developer can understand and contribute to the project.

---

## 5. External Stakeholders and Services

Depending on the final requirements, the platform may interact with external service providers.

Potential examples include:

* Payment providers.
* Email service providers.
* Cloud storage providers.
* Image/media storage services.
* Authentication or identity services.
* Shipping or delivery providers.
* Monitoring services.
* Hosting or infrastructure providers.

These stakeholders will only be included when their services are actually required by the approved system requirements.

---

## 6. Future AI Stakeholders

AI-related stakeholders may become relevant after the initial MVP.

Potential stakeholders include:

* Customers receiving recommendations.
* Artists whose artworks are used by recommendation or similarity systems.
* Platform administrators monitoring AI functionality.
* Developers responsible for AI systems.
* Data/ML engineers.
* AI service or infrastructure providers.

AI stakeholders will be formally evaluated when the future AI expansion is initiated.

| Customers                  | Discover and purchase artworks easily and securely | High      |
| Artists                    | Showcase and sell artworks effectively             | High      |
| Administrators             | Manage and supervise the platform                  | High      |
| Project Owner / Developer  | Build, learn, maintain, and evolve the system      | Very High |
| Future Contributors        | Understand and extend the system                   | Medium    |
| External Service Providers | Provide required platform services                 | Medium    |
| Future AI Stakeholders     | Use, manage, or develop AI capabilities            | Future    |

---

## 8. Stakeholder Management Principles

The project will follow these principles when dealing with stakeholder needs:

1. Stakeholder needs must be understood before defining detailed requirements.
2. Conflicting stakeholder needs must be identified and evaluated.
3. Requirements should provide measurable value to relevant stakeholders.
4. Stakeholder expectations should not automatically become system requirements.
5. Technical decisions should support validated project needs.
6. Scope should remain controlled when stakeholder requests introduce unnecessary complexity.
7. Future stakeholder needs should not unnecessarily increase the initial MVP scope.

---

## 9. Stakeholder Validation

Stakeholders and their needs will be reviewed during the Requirements Engineering phase.

During that phase, the project will determine:

* Detailed stakeholder goals.
* Stakeholder responsibilities.
* User roles.
* User permissions.
* User journeys.
* Stakeholder requirements.
* Conflicting needs.
* Business rules related to stakeholders.

The stakeholder model may therefore change as the project requirements become better understood.
